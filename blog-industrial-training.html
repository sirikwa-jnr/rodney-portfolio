What I Learned During My 2-Month Industrial Training

By Rodney Achota · May – July 2026 · 6 min read

From May 5 to July 3, 2026, I undertook a two-month industrial training at Kaimosi Friends University under the supervision of Dr. Lilian Ronoh. It was one of the most practical experiences of my BSc Information Technology journey — and it changed how I think about technology.

Here's what I learned, module by module.

Module 1: PC Repair & Maintenance

We started with hardware. I learned how to open a computer, identify every component on the motherboard, and diagnose faults from symptoms. A machine that won't power on usually has a power issue. A slow machine usually has RAM, storage or malware problems. Overheating is almost always dust.

I also installed Ubuntu Linux inside a VirtualBox VM on my Windows machine — my first real taste of virtualization. The biggest takeaway: preventive maintenance saves more time than repair.

Module 2: System Administration & the KAFU Data Center

This was eye-opening. We visited the university data center and assessed real production infrastructure:

  • Servers for ERP, Portal, Koha, ODEL and BBB — some of them experienced an outage due to undervoltage
  • Networking equipment with approximately 96% internet availability and ~210 Mbps average speed
  • Storage systems ranging from 1 TB to 3 TB per server
  • Cooling, UPS, voltage regulators, backup generator and solar battery systems
  • CCTV, biometric access and firewalls for security

I learned that a data center is not just about computers — it's about power, cooling, security and backup working together. Daily backups go to KENET and KONSA, with KONSA backing up about 30 minutes after KENET.

Module 3: Database Management Systems

Under Mr. Mutua Jackson, I worked with Microsoft SQL Server Management Studio. I created users, assigned permissions, designed tables with primary and foreign keys, wrote SQL queries, and practiced backup, restoration, monitoring and encryption.

Security is not a feature you add later — it's part of how you design a database from day one.

Module 4: Network Administration & Management

Mdm. Irene introduced us to Cisco Packet Tracer, where I built network topologies, configured IP addresses, and tested device communication. I also learned VLANs, MAC/IP addressing, and — best of all — physically built and terminated Ethernet cables in the lab.

Module 5: Industrial Visit to Nairobi

We visited Computers for Schools Kenya (CFSK) and Strathmore University's iLabAfrica. At CFSK, I learned how e-waste is managed: sorting, recycling, battery recovery, and safe disposal using tools like the "Bulb Eater". At Strathmore, I saw how ICT research becomes real innovation.

Module 6: Web Systems & Technologies

This was my favorite module. Mr. Dennis taught us web hosting, domains, and Content Management Systems — especially Joomla. I built a personal portfolio website using Joomla on XAMPP, running locally at localhost/joomla. That project is what inspired me to build the portfolio you're reading now.

Module 7: Software Development (Mobile Programming)

The final week was all about Android. I installed Android Studio, created my first mobile app, and ran it on my own phone. We also presented group app ideas and peer-evaluated each other — which taught me that feedback is part of building, not an afterthought.

Final Thoughts

Two months, seven modules, one data center, two industrial visits, and a portfolio website. I walked in curious. I walked out with hands-on skills in PC repair, system administration, databases, networking, web development and Android programming.

If you're a student about to start industrial training, my advice is simple: volunteer for the hands-on tasks. That's where the real learning happens.

— Rodney Achota
